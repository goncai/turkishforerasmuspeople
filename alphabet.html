Turkish has 29 letters and every letter is always pronounced the same way — words are read exactly as they are written. There is no Q, W or X. Letters marked tricky sound different from English.

📖 Sound Guide

Ç and Ş
These sounds exist in English, but not as separate letters.
Ç"ch" sound — like chair
Ş"sh" sound — like shoe
Ğ — Soft G
This letter has no direct equivalent in English. It usually lengthens the preceding vowel rather than producing a strong consonant sound.
I / İ Distinction
English has only one "i", but Turkish has two different ones:
İ (dotted)pronounced like "ee" in see
I (dotless ı)a more central, neutral vowel — similar to the vowel in roses without stress
Ö and Ü
These are front rounded vowels. They don't exist in English but are similar to sounds in German:
Ösimilar to German ö — like "ur" in burn, but with rounded lips
Üsimilar to German ü — like saying "ee" with rounded lips
C, J, Y and R — False Friends
These letters look familiar but sound different from English:
Calways "j" as in jam — cami = "jah-mee"
Jsoft "zh" as in measure — rare, mostly in loanwords
Yalways a consonant, like "y" in yes
Rtapped, like Spanish or Italian r — never silent
