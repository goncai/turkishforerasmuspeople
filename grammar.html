1Vowel Harmony

Turkish builds words by adding endings (suffixes). The vowel in the ending changes to match the last vowel of the word. This is called vowel harmony, and it is the reason you see -ler and -lar, or mi, mı, mu and mü.

2-way harmony: e / a
Used by the plural ending -ler / -lar and many others.
Last vowel is a front vowel → -ler
eiöü
Last vowel is a back vowel → -lar
aıou
Examples — plural
4-way harmony: i / ı / u / ü
Used by the question particle mi and by personal endings. Look at the last vowel of the word:
Last vowelEnding vowelExample
a, ııUzak mı? — Is it far?
e, iiGüzel mi? — Is it nice?
o, uuDoğru mu? — Is it correct?
ö, üüBugün mü? — Is it today?

2Yes/No Questions with mi

To turn a sentence into a yes/no question, add mi after the word you are asking about. It is written as a separate word, but it follows vowel harmony (mi / mı / mu / mü). Your voice rises on the word just before it.

Statement → Question
With "I, you, we…"
Personal endings attach to mi, not to the word before it.
PersonQuestionMeaning
benÖğrenci miyim?Am I a student?
senÖğrenci misin?Are you a student?
oÖğrenci mi?Is he/she a student?
bizÖğrenci miyiz?Are we students?
sizÖğrenci misiniz?Are you students? / Are you a student? (formal)
onlarÖğrenciler mi?Are they students?
💡 The position of mi changes the meaning

3Sen or Siz? Informal and Formal "You"

Turkish has two words for "you". Sen is informal and singular. Siz is polite (for one person) and also the plural "you" (for a group). Verbs change with them: -sin for sen, -siniz for siz.

👥 Use sen with…
✅ friends and classmates
✅ your flatmates and other Erasmus students
✅ children
✅ anyone who says "Sen diyebilirsin" (you can say sen)
🎩 Use siz with…
✅ lecturers (together with hocam)
✅ officials at the migration office or student affairs
✅ shop staff, waiters, taxi drivers
✅ older people and anyone you don't know
Same sentence, two levels
EnglishInformal (sen)Formal (siz)
Not sure? Start with siz. Being too polite is never a problem; being too casual can sound rude.

4Var and Yok: "There is" and "I have"

Var means "there is / exists" and yok means "there isn't / doesn't exist". Turkish also uses them for having something: instead of "I have money", you say "my money exists" — Param var. The short answer to a var mı? question is just Var. or Yok.

There is / There isn't
I have / I don't have
-(ı)m = my. para → param (my money), ders → dersim (my class). The vowel follows 4-way harmony.
